Longhorn Rocketry Association

During Fall 2023, I completed a prototype of one of my first design projects, a converging-diverging nozzle for LRA's Taurus II hybrid rocket. The graphite nozzle featured a cylindrical form with a choked throat and diverging exhaust, as well as dual O-ring grooves for proper sealing and fit within the engine casing. This converging-diverging design helps our rocket reach supersonic speeds, while the use of graphite as the nozzle material helps resist extreme heat conditions with high thermal strength and good heat absorption properties.

I attribute LRA for opening my interests in the aerospace industry. Before Fall 2023, I thought of rocketry as a very mystical field with such a strenuous learning curve. After hours spent looking over rocketry design papers and old NASA documentation, I started to feel the allure of the aerospace industry, and I realized I could dive deeper into rocketry with continued effort.

Personal Car Projects

Ever since I was a kid, I’ve been fascinated with automobiles, from sleek exteriors to roaring engines, and everything in between. After officially obtaining my driver’s license, I’ve continually researched automotive components and worked on both my daily driver ('20 Civic Sport) and project car ('97 Integra RS), from everything such as fuel systems, suspension components, and even engine tuning. Pictured above are snapshots that capture some of my latest endeavors, like installing a lightweight billet flywheel and stiffer race clutch for higher torque capacity and lower moment of inertia, as well as a custom ECU with an Hondata S300 board, allowing me to control boost, ignition timing, and fuel tables, with the use of Hondata software.

These projects let me take systems apart, see how every piece fits together, and then test how changes affect performance. Each upgrade grows my understanding of the balance between mechanical hardware, electronics, and calibrated software, while strengthening my passion for engineering and cars.

I kicked off the build cycle by overhauling our design and simulation workflow. After inheriting the Frame Lead role as a freshman and having an unoptimized FEA setup in place, I focused on breaking down complex failure points with more targeted breakout sims. I also used sweep meshing and the 3-2-1 support method for more reliable results. This helped speed up runtimes and make it easier to pinpoint areas of high stress with a more conservative FEA setup.

While taking a MATLAB class in the fall, I saw an opportunity to apply what I was learning to my Frame simulation work, by writing a script to generate stiffness matrices from tube geometric and material properties. This let us simulate only the occupant cell while mimicking the rest of the chassis with body-ground joints, cutting runtime by over 260% without sacrificing accuracy (from multiple hours to sub one).
